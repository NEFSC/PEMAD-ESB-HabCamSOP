Fiber Optic Termination

Purpose

Underwater fiber optic cables are used in environments where standard communication cables cannot provide the required durability, data capacity, or long-term reliability. The underwater sensors, subsea equipment, and HabCam need a cable that can handle water exposure, pulling force, bending, abrasion, pressure, and repeated deployment.

On the Henry B. Bigelow specifically, the fiber optic cable (run through the winch cable) is cut yearly, so a new fiber optic termination is required every time before deployment for a new survey.



Hardware

Part 1: Inserting the Fiber Optic into the Clevis

  1. Mark and cut about 10m (33ft) off of the ship’s fiber optic cable with a grinder and cutting wheel; this process disposes of any wire that was exposed to additional stress by going up and around the hanging block. Prior to cutting, put a hose clamp just after the 33ft mark (down the wire) to ensure that the cable does not start to unwrap.
  2. After cutting the wire, leave the hose clamp on the open end and pay out enough wire length to reach into the starboard side of the ship’s CTD lab and onto a table, where the fiber optic connectors will be applied.
  3. Measure and mark 24ft from the open end. Install another hose clamp on this end.
  4. Down at the 24 ft mark, take a dremel and score the outermost shield wire. (Note: There are 3 layers of shielding).
  5. Starting from the open end, start peeling back the outer shield wire until you have reached the scored mark. Once you reach that mark, the shield wire should break off.
  6. Repeat step 5 for the remaining shield wires until the black plastic shield is exposed.
  7. Starting at the 24 ft mark, measure 12 inches up the wire and make a mark with a sharpie. (Note: This is 25 ft from open end of the cable).
  8. Remove hose clamps and insert wire through the narrow end of sandblasted clevis and pull through until the 12 inch mark is visible on the wider end of the clevis.
  9. Refasten hose clamp only at the 12 inch mark.


Part 2: Creating the Broom

Steel Armor Wire Broom Formed and Seated inside Clevis Well

  1. Starting at the 24 ft mark, start peeling the strands of shield wire (all three layers) until you reach the hose clamp (12 inches up the wire).
  2. Bend each wire at a 90 degree angle against the hose clamp. Try to evenly space the wires around the cable. (All the wires will form a “broom”).
  3. The black rubber part of the cable should now be visible up to this mark.
  4. Remove the hose clamp at the 25ft mark and move it right after the smaller opening of the clevis. (This prevents the shield wires from unwinding/bird-caging up the cable).
  5. Walk the clevis out to the back deck.
  6. Install two screw eyes in the deck, 6 feet from each other.
  7. Take a 2 ft strap. Then feed a 1in diameter pin through the two holes at the opening of the clevis. Make sure one end of the strap is fed through the pin.
  8. Attach the other side of the strap to the screw eye on deck with a 5/8th shackle.
  9. Attach a Yale Grip rope line down the cable just after the clevis using several half hitches.
  10. Put a 5/8th shackle on the rope end that is going away from the open end of the cable. This shackle will attach to the ship’s manual come-along winch and pull the broom into the clevis’ well.
  11. Secure the come-along winch and place the shackle that is on the half-hitched rope (holding the cable) into the come-along winch hook. Make sure the come-along, rope, and cables are arranged in a straight line.
  12. Attach the shackle to the other screw eye.
  13. Use the come-along to pull down the broom into the clevis well. Try to get about 4-6 inches of broom inside the well; it is important, as we do not want the termination slipping during deployment, even with epoxy.
  14. Once the broom is fully seated in the clevis, move and tighten the hose clamp to the cable at the base of the clevis (end with small opening).


Part 3: Epoxy

  1. Hang the termination by the clevis bolt holes so that the broom is facing up.
  2. Take 15ft of Tygon tubing and slide it over the black wire. Attach the tubing to the black rubber wire, and move it so that one end of the tubing is inserted down into the epoxy well. This tubing protects the black rubber wire from trimmed metal ends, which will occur later.
  3. Try to bend the broom ends away from the Tygon tubing as much as possible, so that they will not poke into it. (Note: Bend the black cable at a 45 degree angle so you do not block the pathway of the load pin).
  4. Using splicing tape, followed by electrical tape, create a reservoir around 2-3 in high around the openings of the clevis, where the broom becomes exposed.
  5. Place newspaper or cardboard underneath where the clevis is hanging to prevent any epoxy mixture from spilling onto the ship’s floors.
  6. You will need gloves, Socketfast resin and its catalyst, a zip tie, and a long wooden mixing stick. Put on your gloves, take your catalyst and pour it into the Socketfast resin. Immediately take a wooden mixing stick and mix epoxy thoroughly for at least 2 minutes; the resin container should feel warm.
  7. Carefully pour epoxy into the clevis well until the mixture comes up to the top of the duct tape reservoir. Use a funnel if available. A zip tie can be used to guide the flow and pop bubbles as the epoxy is poured. (Tip: Lightly tap the clevis with a hammer as the resin is being poured to allow bubbles to rise).
  8. While the epoxy is wet, bend back any remaining broom strand ends away from the Tygon tubing.
  9. Let the epoxy stand for a minimum of 1-2 hours before trimming back the broom strands to be as flush as possible with the hardened epoxy.
    *Important: If left to harden at room temperature, the epoxy should set for at least 24 hours before deploying into water. If time is of the essence, you may uniformly heat the clevis at 180-200° F with two heat guns for about 30 minutes to fully cure it.
  10. Before attaching the clevis to the HabCam towing bridle, perform a pull test with the ship’s winch and the towing block to ensure that the epoxy will hold under tension. Aim for a tension of 8,000 lbs.


Part 4: The Boot

  1. The boot uses 6 rolls of 2in self-vulcanizing tape and 2-3 layers (not rolls!) of 2in electrical tape.
  2. Be sure when applying the tape that you pull tightly to ensure that the tape stretches and adheres to itself properly.
  3. Taper self-vulcanizing tape so that the area closest to the clevis is thickest.
  4. Entire boot length will be about 2.5ft or just under, measured from where the grooves in the clevis are.


Part 5: Fairing on Cable

Applying Zip Tie Fairing along Tow Cable on Deck

  1. Use the wide, 18” zip ties.
  2. Starting from where the boot ends, apply zip ties along the wire for about 15-20 feet until 8-10 bags of 100 zip ties are consumed.
  3. Fairing wrap could be used as a substitute if handy; secure the wrap with electrical tape.


Fiber Optics Power Terminals

Part 1: Preparing Power and Fiber Optic Wires

  1. From the open end of the fiber optic wire, measure 2 feet down and make a mark. VERY CAREFULLY take a razor knife and score the black rubber down the length of the wire to that mark; you do not need to cut all the way through the rubber to remove it. Use pliers to peel back the stiff rubber before cutting it off at the 2-foot mark.
    (Tip: Squeezing the end of the wire with pliers can help split the rubber).
  2. After the 2 feet of black rubber has been removed, carefully separate the exposed wires. You should have 3 power wires (2 brown, 1 green), 3 fiber optic wires (1 red, 1 gray, 1 black), flexible rubber spacing material, and a thin, white-gray spacer strand.
  3. Remove the flexible rubber spacing material and cut the thin white-gray spacer strand.
  4. Snip about an inch off the fiber and power wires to eliminate any broken regions resulting from initial cable cuts.


Part 2: Inserting the Fiber Optic Wire into the J-Box

  1. Unscrew the threaded cap of the empty connector on the side of the J-Box, removing the metal ring collar and rubber gasket.

Figure 2.1: Open J-Box Interior showing Empty Connector Port and Terminal Block


  1. Guide the power and fiber optic wires through the threaded cap (threads facing the open end), followed by the metal ring collar and the rubber gasket (flattest part facing the open wire end). *DO NOT MISS THIS STEP!
  2. Guide the wires through the open connector until about half an inch of black rubber is visible inside the J-Box. Secure a hose clamp around the black rubber flush against the inner wall to prevent slipping.

Threading Cap, Collar & Rubber Gasket onto Wire
Internal Hose Clamp Secured Flush to Inner Wall


  1. Secure the gasket, metal ring collar, and threaded cap to the J-Box. Tighten the cap firmly with a pipe wrench without twisting the connector body.

Seating Rubber Gasket & Metal Collar into Connector
Tightening Threaded Cap with Pipe Wrench


Part 3: Connecting Ring Terminals to the Power Wires

  1. Locate the 2 thick brown wires (ground/neutral) and 1 thick green wire (power driver).
  2. Strip less than ½” of insulation from the green wire using a 12-10 AWG wire stripper.

12-10 AWG Ring Terminal and Gauged Wire Stripper/Crimping Tool


  1. Place a yellow 12-10 AWG ring terminal over the exposed copper wire.
  2. Crimp the upper half of the yellow jacket securely using the crimping tool. Tug on the terminal to verify connection strength.

Crimped 12-10 AWG Yellow Ring Terminal


  1. Repeat steps 2–4 for both brown wires.


Part 4: Connecting the Power Wires to the J-Box

  1. Connect the green power wire across from the center red terminal screw inside the J-Box. Gently loop the wire around the interior avoiding sharp 90° bends.

Green Power Wire Connected to Center Terminal Screw


  1. Attach the brown wires to either side of the green wire across from their corresponding red terminal counterparts.

Green and Brown Conductors Connected to J-Box Terminal Strip



Fiber Optic ST Connectors

Part 1: Safety, Cleanliness, and Pre-Heating Oven

  1. Wear safety glasses AT ALL TIMES when handling fiber optic wire ends.
  2. Clean all tools and work surfaces with isopropyl alcohol and lint-free wipes prior to stripping or polishing.
  3. Plug in and turn on the 3M Hot Melt oven kit; allow at least 6 minutes for uniform heating.


Part 2: Exposing the Protective Jacketing

  1. Unpack a 3M Hot Melt Singlemode ST Yellow Boot connector packet. Discard the thin plastic tube.

3M ST Yellow Boot Packet & Disassembled Component Parts


  1. Slide the yellow boot narrow-end first onto the gray fiber optic wire.

Yellow Boot Slid onto Gray Fiber Wire


  1. Measure cut lengths on the 3M oven hotplate scale guide and mark the wire.

Measuring Cut Lengths on 3M Hotplate Scale Guide


Part 3: Trimming Metal Strands

  1. Fan out the metal jacketing strands evenly without bending the central buffered glass strand.
  2. Fold the metal strands back along the gray wire jacket as flat as possible.

Fanning Out Metal Jacketing Strands
Bending Metal Strands Backward Flat


  1. Trim the folded metal strands to approximately 1/4 inch in length.


Part 4: Removing the 1st Buffer Layer

  1. Reference the hotplate guide to determine buffer removal length.
  2. Clean a 3-hole stripper tool with alcohol. Using the middle hole, gently strip away the primary buffer layer in short, straight pulls. Clean the tool after every strip.


Part 5: Removing the Final Buffer Layer

  1. Using the smallest hole on the stripper tool, gently remove the inner buffer coating until flush with the thick buffer.

3-Hole Fiber Optic Stripper Tool
Bare Glass Fiber Strand After Stripping


  1. Gently wipe the bare glass strand with an alcohol-moistened low-lint wipe to remove microscopic buffer debris.


Part 6: Preparing to Heat the ST Connector

  1. Remove cap from ST connector and place connector ferrule-side down into an oven ST holder.
  2. Confirm the oven is fully heated (~6 minutes).


Part 7: Inserting Fiber into ST Connector

  1. Place an ST holder into the hot oven hole while monitoring with a stopwatch.

Placing ST Connector Ferrule into ST Holder
3M Hot Melt Oven & Hotplate Setup


  1. Observe internal blue epoxy liquefy (~60–90 seconds). When epoxy reaches near the top of the ferrule hole, transfer holder to hotplate and immediately insert fiber strand until metal strands click flush into the connector.
  2. Allow assembly to cool for 3 minutes undisturbed.


Part 8: Removing ST Connector from Holder

  1. Slide yellow boot over connector rear housing until it snaps in place.
  2. Pull boot straight up to remove connector from holder.
  3. Verify optical continuity using a visual fault locator (red laser).


Part 9: Scoring and Removing Fiber Nipple

  1. Position ruby scribe perpendicular to fiber strand 1/16th inch above ferrule tip.

Scribing Fiber Perpendicularly One Fiber Diameter from Ferrule Tip


  1. Gently score the glass strand and pull straight off. Discard glass tip in sharps container.


Part 10: Air Polishing

  1. Perform 15–20 figure-8 passes on a 9-micron gray polishing film until glass stub is flush with blue epoxy bead.


Part 11: Pad Polishing with Green Film

  1. Clean black rubber polishing pad with alcohol. Place wet 1-micron green lapping film on pad.
  2. Mount ferrule into polishing puck and perform figure-8 rotations until blue epoxy turns clear and white ceramic ferrule is exposed.

Failed Scope Views: Chipped Core, Epoxy Rings & Scratches
Dirty Ferrule View (Clean with Alcohol Wipe)


  1. Inspect ferrule endface under 200x fiber scope to verify clean core surface.


Part 12: Final Polish with White Film

  1. Perform 2–3 light figure-8 passes on 0.05-micron white film for final surface finish.

Ideal/Clean Fiber Ferrule Core View under Viewing Scope


Part 13: Terminating Backup Fibers

  1. Repeat Parts 2–12 for the black and red backup optical fiber lines.


Part 14: Connecting Main Fiber to J-Box Cable

  1. Connect primary gray fiber ST connector to the metal optical coupler inside the J-Box.

J-Box Layout and Cable Entry Positioning on Worktable


Coupling Primary Gray ST Fiber to Metal Adapter Assembly (Left: Aligning Connector; Right: Mated in Position)


  1. Coil red and black backup fibers neatly inside J-Box housing and secure loosely with zip ties.

Red and Black Backup Fibers Coiled and Zip-Tied inside J-Box



Buttoning Up the J-Box

Part 1: Securing the Urethane Bladder

  1. Align yellow urethane bladder indentation face-down against J-Box rim.
  2. Align metal retaining frame over bladder.
  3. Thread 5mm Allen screws with lock and flat washers through frame and bladder.

1. Aligning Yellow Bladder Indentation Face Down
2. Metal Retaining Plate Alignment

3. 5mm Allen Screw with Lock & Flat Washers
4. Threading 5mm Allen Screws into Perimeter


  1. Evenly torque perimeter screws in a cross-pattern using 5mm Allen wrench until snug without over-compressing bladder material.


Part 2: Filling J-Box with Compensating Oil

  1. Connect oil fill tube to lower J-Box quick-disconnect valve.
  2. Connect purge tube from upper valve to overflow collection bucket.
  3. Pump dielectric compensating oil into J-Box until all air bubbles are purged.

Compensating Oil Vacuum Pump & Purge Reservoir Setup


Part 3: Securing J-Box to HabCam Vehicle

  1. Route oil-filled J-Box and main tow cable along the inside/rear of towing bridle structure.
  2. Mount J-Box inverted on port-aft frame rail.
  3. Connect optical plug to telemetry bottle optical bulkhead port; connect power plug to telemetry bottle power port.
